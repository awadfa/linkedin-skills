## Draft 1 — Price vs investment quality

A lower asking price doesn't automatically mean a better property investment in Dubai.

Before comparing two opportunities, I look at the full picture: entry price, service charges, realistic rental demand, exit liquidity, developer or building track record, and the investor's time horizon.

Two units at similar prices can carry very different risks and ownership costs.

My approach is simple: understand the investor's objective first, then evaluate the asset against it.

What matters most to you when assessing a property investment: income, appreciation, or flexibility?

*Evidence status:* General professional commentary. No numerical market claims.

## Draft 2 — Reading quarterly market data

Dubai's real estate market generates a lot of headlines. But a headline number rarely tells the whole story.

When reviewing quarterly transaction statistics, I separate sales from mortgages, transaction counts from transaction values, and different property categories.

I also check the reporting period, data definitions and whether the figures have been revised. Comparing unlike datasets can produce a convincing but misleading conclusion.

For investors, good decisions start with understanding what the data actually measures.

*Evidence status:* Methodological commentary only. **Do not insert any transaction figures** until original Data Dubai metadata and currency/coverage have been checked.

## Draft 3 — A practical referral partnership

The strongest real estate partnerships start with clarity, not a catalogue of listings.

If you're advising investors considering Dubai, the useful starting point is their budget, preferred asset class, target holding period, expected income needs and tolerance for risk.
